Youth Community Leadership Initiative

A UN youth leadership programme, seen from three seats: poster designer, volunteer, and author of a community needs assessment.

Civic & Public Campaigns
Part One The Programme

YCLI trains young people to lead in their own communities, using WHO's Healthy Settings Approach. It runs as five phases: two residential trainings of eight days each, a community needs assessment between them, then action planning and implementation, then policy papers and youth parliamentary debates. I took part in Cohort 02.

Phase 1: group work using the Healthy Settings framework, ahead of the needs assessment.

Phase 3: asking a question on community engagement in a plenary with Cohort 02.

Phase 1 team: eight days of residential training, including the initial conflict mapping.

Phase 3: back with the cohort to consolidate what we learned and plan interventions.

Part Two In the Field

Between the two trainings came the community needs assessment. My area was Aranayake Division, Kegalle District, in Sabaragamuwa Province. These are photographs from the first field visit, taken by me. Drag or swipe sideways for more.

Part Three From Assessment to Action

Phase 4 is where the programme turns assessment into action: each team drafts a community action plan and carries out selected interventions. Ours helped with a classroom and with water taps, which puts the work under two Sustainable Development Goals: SDG 4, Quality Education, and SDG 6, Clean Water and Sanitation. The written record of the assessment behind it is mine. Read the Community Needs Assessment.

Separate Commission The Recruitment Poster

Separate from my place in the cohort, UNV Sri Lanka asked me to design the call for applicants. Shown here as a mockup. The final ran in English, Sinhala and Tamil, and all three are on WHO Sri Lanka's YCLI page.

The Youth Community Leadership Initiative (YCLI) is a UN programme delivered by WHO Sri Lanka, UNDP and UNV with the National Youth Services Council and the Ministry of Youth and Sports, supported by the UN Peacebuilding Fund. It uses health as a way in: the Healthy Settings Approach gives young people from different backgrounds a shared language for improving their own communities. WHO's own write-up covers the programme in full.

I was a participant, not UN staff. I took part in Cohort 02 as a community development volunteer, and this page shows what I did from inside the programme, plus one piece of design work that sits alongside it.

The programme asks its trainees to do something most leadership courses don't: leave the training room and assess a real community. The structure is two eight-day residential trainings, ten modules each, with a community needs assessment in between. My team's area was Aranayake Division in Kegalle District. I went into the field, took the photographs, and wrote the Community Needs Assessment, covering the division's social fabric, its environmental assets and its economic gaps.

Phase 4 is where assessment becomes action. Teams draft a community action plan and carry out selected interventions. Ours helped with a classroom and with water taps: education and clean water, or SDG 4 and SDG 6 in the language of the Sustainable Development Goals.

The recruitment poster was a separate commission. UNV Sri Lanka asked me to design the call for applicants, and for most people it was the first thing they saw of YCLI. It tells you who can apply (Sri Lankan youth aged 18 to 32), what you get (a one-year, fully funded programme with 20 training modules) and the deadline (5 December 2021). It ran in English, Sinhala and Tamil.
